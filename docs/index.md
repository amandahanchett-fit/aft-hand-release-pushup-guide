# Hand Release Push Up Guide for the Army Fitness Test

The hand release push up is the first event of the Army Fitness Test, and a clean rep is easier to earn than most soldiers expect. This guide explains the movement, the common reasons a rep is not counted, and a simple four week plan to add repetitions without hurting your shoulders.

## What the standard asks for

Start face down with your chest, hips and thighs on the ground, then place your hands flat beside your chest. Push up until your arms are straight and your body forms one line from head to heels. Lower yourself back down, lift both hands off the ground for a moment, then place them again and begin the next rep. You have two minutes to complete as many valid reps as you can, and resting is only allowed in the up position.

## Why reps are not counted

Most failed reps come from the same few faults. Hips sag or rise ahead of the chest, the elbows do not lock at the top, the chest does not touch the ground, or the hands stay on the floor during the release. Another common fault is stopping with the knees on the ground. Film a set from the side and you will spot these faults within seconds.

## Build a steady rhythm

Many people start fast and fall apart after forty seconds. Aim for a pace you can hold for the whole two minutes, such as one rep every three seconds for the first minute, then adjust. Breathe in on the way down and out on the way up. Short pauses at the top, with locked arms, can be used to recover before the next descent.

## Four week plan

Train the movement three days a week with at least one rest day between sessions.

Week one: do six sets of five clean reps with sixty seconds of rest. Focus on form and a full release.

Week two: do five sets of eight clean reps with seventy five seconds of rest.

Week three: do four sets of ten reps, then one set to near failure, with ninety seconds of rest.

Week four: do a practice two minute test on day one, rest on day two, and train lightly on day three.

## Support work

Add scapular push ups, incline push ups for volume, and slow tempo reps on a second day. Strong triceps and a stable core carry over to every other event, because a tight body position is the same one used in the plank. Stretch the chest and shoulders after each session, and keep your wrists warm with a few circles before you start.

## Mistakes to avoid

Do not chase numbers with half reps, because the grader will not count them and you will teach your body the wrong pattern. Do not train to failure every day, since tired shoulders lose control quickly. Do not forget to eat enough protein and sleep well, since the muscle gains happen during recovery.

## Track your progress

After each timed practice test, write down your total and compare it with your age band. You can enter your reps in the AFT Calculator to see the points they earn and decide whether to focus on this event or another one next.
